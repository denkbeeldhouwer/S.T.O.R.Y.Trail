# The Thirty-Degree Walk

**A living prototype of a hero's journey route through the more-than-human world**

*Session proposal — Wild Pedagogies Gathering 2026*
*Format: excursion / moving experience in nature · 2–2.5 hours*
*Timing: strong preference for Day 1 and/or the final morning — see below*
*Facilitator: Guido Crolla (Denkbeeldhouwer, Arnhem)*

---

## What this is

I am building a walking route through Arnhem: 9.5 kilometres from the central station to the two campuses at the other end of the city, with twelve living willow land-art installations along the way. Each installation marks one station of the hero's journey. The willow grows, is pruned by students and neighbours, and changes with the seasons. The route is designed, tested as a concept, and not yet in the ground.

At Quadenoord I would like to walk it before it exists.

## What we do

We walk a loop in the woods around Het Paradijs and stop twelve times. At each stop the group physically turns thirty degrees. Twelve stops, three hundred and sixty degrees, one circle: we end where we began, facing the same direction, seeing something else.

At each stop three things happen. The place is given its station — the call, the refusal, the threshold, the ordeal, the return. A question is asked that belongs to that station. And the group makes one small gesture from what is already there: a bent branch, a stone laid down, a mark that will disappear.

The mentor on this route is not a person. It is whatever meets you at the fourth stop — a tree, a slope, a bird, the wind changing. That is the wager of the walk: that the more-than-human world is perfectly capable of playing every role in the oldest story we have, if we stop assigning them to each other.

## What it opens

The route in Arnhem runs from a railway station, through parks and neighbourhoods, to two institutions of higher education. It crosses the city, and it crosses the wall between school and world. It has to be permitted, planted, pruned and inherited by the next cohort of students. It is an ecology of learning that grows inside an existing institutional structure and slowly changes what that structure is for.

I bring this walk to the gathering as an unfinished thing. I want to know what happens when it is walked by people who think about wild pedagogies for a living, and what they would do differently. Whatever the group leaves behind at the twelve stops travels back to Arnhem with me.

## When

I would like to propose this walk twice, and the two versions are not the same session.

**Monday 26 October, 15:00–17:00** — as the opening immersive experience. Walked on the first afternoon, the twelve stations become a projection screen for the days ahead: the call you are answering by being here, the threshold you crossed to come, the ordeal you did not plan for yet. The group arrives at Quadenoord and walks its own gathering before it happens.

**Friday 30 October, morning** — as the closing. Walked on the last day, the same twelve stations become a mirror. What called you, what you refused, where the turn came, what you are carrying home. The route ends where it started, which is what a closing circle does anyway; the walk could lead into it or be it.

Doing both would be the strongest version. The same ground, the same twelve stops, four days apart — and everyone finds out for themselves how far thirty degrees can move a person.

## Practical

Any weather. Sturdy footwear. Two to two and a half hours. Works well with up to twenty walkers, and works with the whole gathering if we split into groups that set off at intervals and meet at the final stop. No materials needed — the woods provide them. I bring the map, the questions, and a pruning knife.
